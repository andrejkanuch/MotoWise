# Onboard lens — iteration 4 (research only, nothing edited)

Scope: `lens/SCOPE.md` (root-only routes now under `src/app/(root)/`). Method: source read of every first-run surface, `curl` of `http://localhost:3000` with desktop, iPhone and Android user agents (no browser pane), `messages/en.json` for every key quoted. Frame: the web's job is to turn a visitor into an install (and, for Pro, a web purchase); the signed-in garage is read-only ("the web displays, the app does"); 85% of installs never return after day one, so every first-run screen must set the right expectation and leave exactly one next step. Paths are `apps/web/src/` unless noted. ★ = the 8 most important rows.

Verified by curl: `/get` sends an iPhone UA `307 → apps.apple.com/…id6760291360`, an Android UA `307 → play.google.com/…&referrer=utm_source%3Dinstagram…`, a desktop UA gets the QR page (200). `/welcome` and `/garage` redirect signed-out visitors to `/login?redirect=…`. `/ride/x`, `/rider/x`, `/u/x/saved`, `/t/x` are real 404s; `/share/x` is a 200 "This link has expired" page. `/explore/search?q=zzzz` is a 200 with the no-results state.

## Flow verdicts

**1 · Landing → install — passes the 5-second test on desktop, half-passes on a phone.** `Every mile, on record.` + `Rides, service and costs — one logbook for every bike in your garage. Free, and built by a rider.` + `Free · iOS + Android · Built for motorcycles` says what, why and that it is free inside the first screen. The next step exists at the hero, the nav (`Get the app` → `/#download`), the closing CTA and the sticky bar (Android + desktop). Three gaps: the store buttons never match the device (an Android phone gets a copper `Download for iOS` and a ghost literal `Google Play`, in the hero, the closing CTA and the garage); on a phone the Bike Plate sits between the description and the buttons, so on a 667px screen the CTA is below the fold and, in the EU, under a ~160px cookie bar; and on desktop every "unknown platform" CTA opens the App Store web page in a new tab instead of `/get`, the page that was built for desktop (QR + both stores). `/get` itself is right: phones never see a page, desktop sees one headline, three benefits, both stores and a QR.

**2 · Sign-up → welcome → garage — the screens are good, the two things between the screens are not.** `/signup` promises `Start your logbook. / Free forever for logging. Pro removes the limits.`, asks email + password + confirm password, and after submit says `Check your email. / Open the link in the email to finish creating your account.` with resend and change-address. But the email a web sign-up receives is Supabase's default (`Confirm your signup / Follow this link to confirm your user: / Confirm your mail`; the branded template only covers the app's code flow), and an expired or reused link bounces to `/login` with no message. `/welcome` (`Signed in. Now get the app on your phone.`, three reasons, QR + copy link ≥768, one copper `Get the app` <768, `Sign in with the same account: …`) is the right handoff; its one explanatory sentence (`Welcome.subtitle`) exists in en.json but is not rendered. The empty garage is a model empty state: `Your garage · No bikes yet`, `Add your bike in the app: make, model, year.`, QR on desktop / store buttons on phone, the account to sign in with, then the reasons. `/pro` explains free before money (`Logging stays free.`, the free rows first in the table); checkout success says `Pro is active` and offers `Open your garage` — but never says the one thing that matters: open the app and sign in with the same account.

**3 · Shared links as first touch — works for `/t`, `/rider` and `/u`; `/ride` and `/share` leave the stranger guessing.** Every share page carries the brand mark, a condensed title, figures and a closing pitch with a store action. `/t/<token>` is the best: `Ride it with them / Open the trip in the app to see the route on the map, join the group and record the ride.`, a deep link on phones, a dedicated not-found (`This trip isn't available`). `/ride/<id>` opens with a ride name and a date and says what MotoVault is only in the footer; a private or deleted ride (and an unknown rider) gets the site-wide `Wrong turn. This road doesn't exist. Nothing at /ride/…`. `/share/<token>` names the bike and the records but both `Get the app` buttons link to the home page, not a store. Nobody is named as the sender on `/ride` and `/share` (the queries carry no rider), which is acceptable; the recipient still needs "what is this" in the first line.

**4 · Explore as first touch — pitch present, not blocking, and "copy it into the app" is explained, but late.** `/explore` closes with `Take the route with you / Open any route in the app, ride it, and keep the record.`; region pages carry no pitch (right: the list and map come first). The trip page's hero has a copper `Get the app` with no reason attached; the reason (`Copy it into your trip planner, change the dates and stops, then ride it. Free.`) only appears in the end CTA and the <1024 sticky bar. A region that exists in the taxonomy but has no routes yet renders `No routes match these filters / Clear a filter…` with no filter set.

**5 · Empty states and zero data — all but two say what happened and the next step.** Garage: no service (`No service scheduled. Add the first interval in the app and it counts down from your odometer.` + `Open in the app`), no rides (`No rides yet. Record your next ride. Only the phone has GPS.`), nothing logged (`Nothing logged in 2026. Snap a receipt in the app and it fills itself in.`), no public username (`Set up your rider profile … in the MotoVault app` + `Do this in the MotoVault app →`), search (`No routes for "x"` + `Try a shorter search…` + `Browse all routes`), blog (`No articles match "x".` + `Clear the search`), 404 (`Wrong turn.` + `Where to instead`). The two misses: the zero-route region above, and `/u/<handle>/saved` with nothing saved (`@x hasn't saved any routes.` — no next step).

**6 · Labels — only `Pro` and `Editor's pick` assume prior knowledge.** `logbook` is first used in the hero description next to `Rides, service and costs` (self-explaining) and again on `/signup`; `GPX` first appears as `Multi-day routes and GPX export` and `Export a GPX to the sat-nav` (explained in context; FAQ 11 covers the rest); `twist score` never surfaces — the route card prints `Twisty` / `Very twisty`; `clone` never surfaces — the trip page says `Saved by riders` and `Copy it into your planner`; `Bike Plate` is only an accessible region name (`Bike plate: Ready`) — the plate itself reads on its own. `Pro` stands alone in the signed-out nav with no noun, and `Editor's pick` names an editor the visitor has never met.

**7 · Cookie consent — plain, equal, non-blocking by construction; heavy on a phone.** The bar is client-mounted, EU-only, bottom-anchored, so it never blocks SSR content; the copy (`One required cookie keeps you signed in. Three optional ones remember your settings, count which features riders open, and record a crash so we can fix it. Nothing goes to ad networks.`) is honest and short; `Only required` is a ghost button the same size as the copper `Accept all`. On a 390px phone the bar is three lines plus three actions (~160px) and, on Android, the sticky app bar stacks on top of it: two bars before the headline. In the settings sheet, two of the three switches are inert (`Save choices` reads only the analytics switch).
