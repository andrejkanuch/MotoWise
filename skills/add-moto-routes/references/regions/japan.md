### Hokkaido Panorama Road — Biei to Furano Patchwork Hills
Meta: JP | JP-01 | Biei | 1d | 60km | easy | paved | 450m↑ | pick:yes
Start: 43.5933, 142.4640
Description: The rolling patchwork farmland of central Hokkaido at its most photogenic. The hills around Biei are carpeted in lavender, sunflowers, wheat, and potato fields arranged in geometric colour blocks. Late July–August is peak season. Quiet rural roads, no hairpins — pure pastoral beauty.

Waypoints:
1. [start | d0 | morning] Biei Station (43.5933, 142.4640) — Start at Biei. No fuel on the patchwork roads — fill up here.
2. [photo | d0 | morning] Ken & Mary's Tree (43.5640, 142.4197) — Famous lone poplar made iconic by a 1972 Nissan Skyline ad. Still standing, always photographed.
3. [scenic | d0 | morning] Patchwork Hill Viewpoint (43.5720, 142.4353) — Classic Hokkaido panorama: rolling multicolour fields stretching to the Tokachi peaks.
4. [scenic | d0 | morning] Mild Seven Hill (43.5480, 142.3891) — Row of elms along a ridge, immortalised in a Mild Seven cigarette ad. Quintessential Hokkaido scene.
5. [food | d0 | afternoon] Farm Tomita Lavender Fields (43.5343, 142.3897) — Hokkaido's most famous lavender farm. Lavender ice cream and dried flower souvenirs. Magnificent in late July.
6. [end | d0 | afternoon] Furano (43.3504, 142.3834) — End in Furano ski resort town. Good restaurants and onsen.

### Hokkaido Shiretoko Pass — World Heritage Wilderness
Meta: JP | JP-01 | Utoro | 1d | 70km | challenging | paved | 690m↑ | pick:yes
Start: 44.0726, 144.9937
Description: The Shiretoko Pass (738m) crosses the spine of Japan's most remote UNESCO World Heritage peninsula, where brown bears outnumber people. The road closes in winter and opens only in late April. Dramatic ocean views on both the Okhotsk and Pacific sides, dense virgin forest, and almost zero traffic.

Waypoints:
1. [start | d0 | morning] Utoro | Shiretoko Five Lakes (44.0726, 144.9937) — Start in Utoro. Shiretoko Five Lakes boardwalk is a worthy morning detour before the pass.
2. [scenic | d0 | morning] Shiretoko Pass Summit (738m) (44.0136, 144.8878) — The highest point. Views down both sides. Bears frequently spotted on the slopes below — do not leave your bike unattended.
3. [photo | d0 | morning] Rausu Lookout (44.0500, 145.1000) — First views of the Rausu side — the wilder, less-visited east coast dropping to the Kunashiri Strait.
4. [food | d0 | afternoon] Rausu Fishing Port (44.0211, 145.2011) — End road stop. Fresh crab and salmon from one of Hokkaido's most productive fishing harbours.
5. [end | d0 | afternoon] Rausu (44.0211, 145.2011) — End at Rausu. Return via pass or ferry to Hokkaido mainland.

### Noto Peninsula Outer Coast — Kongo Coast Road
Meta: JP | JP-17 | Kanazawa | 2d | 340km | moderate | paved | 1800m↑ | pick:yes
Start: 36.5613, 136.6562
Description: Two days circumnavigating the Noto Peninsula, Ishikawa Prefecture's arm jutting into the Sea of Japan. The Kongo Coast Road on the west runs through dramatic cliff scenery and ancient fishing villages. Wajima is famous for its lacquerware (Wajima-nuri), and the tip of the peninsula feels genuinely untouched by modernity.

Waypoints:
1. [start | d0 | morning] Kanazawa (36.5613, 136.6562) — Start in Kanazawa (Kenroku-en garden, samurai district). Head north on coastal Route 249.
2. [scenic | d0 | morning] Chirihama Driveable Beach (36.8570, 136.7210) — 8km of firm sand that acts as a public road. Japan's only legal beach drive — surreal.
3. [photo | d0 | afternoon] Ganmon Rock Arch (37.3167, 136.7833) — The gate-shaped sea arch on the Kongo Coast, eroded by waves over millennia. Pull-off with sea spray.
4. [scenic | d0 | afternoon] Senmaida Rice Terraces (37.3733, 136.8817) — 1,004 tiny rice paddies cascading down the cliff to the sea. Japan's most photographed rice terrace. Stunning at sunset.
5. [overnight | d0 | evening] Wajima (37.3997, 136.8989) — Overnight in Wajima. Known for lacquerware and the morning Asa-ichi market. Ryokan with seafood kaiseki dinner.
6. [food | d1 | morning] Wajima Morning Market (37.3990, 136.8984) — Start Day 2 at the 1,000-year-old morning market. Local vendors sell pickles, seafood, and crafts. Open 8–11am.
7. [scenic | d1 | morning] Suzu Cape (37.4382, 137.2629) — The tip of the Noto Peninsula. Lighthouse and dramatic Sea of Japan views. Quiet and windswept.
8. [photo | d1 | afternoon] Mitsukejima Rock Island (37.4667, 137.2167) — An island only accessible at low tide, connected to the beach by a sand bar. Otherworldly.
9. [fuel | d1 | afternoon] Anamizu (37.2333, 136.9000) — Fuel here — limited options on the eastern Noto coast.
10. [end | d1 | afternoon] Kanazawa (36.5613, 136.6562) — Return to Kanazawa completing the Noto circuit.

### Izu Peninsula — Nishi-Izu Skyline & Dogashima Coast
Meta: JP | JP-22 | Ito | 1d | 130km | moderate | paved | 1650m↑ | pick:no
Start: 34.9647, 139.1013
Description: The Izu Peninsula south of Tokyo offers the best coastal riding near Japan's capital. The Nishi-Izu Skyline runs along mountain ridges with views of the Pacific on one side and Mt Fuji on the other on clear days. The Dogashima coast has dramatic sea caves and cliffs eroded into bizarre formations.

Waypoints:
1. [start | d0 | morning] Ito (34.9647, 139.1013) — Start in Ito on the east Izu coast. Onsen town. Head south then cut across to the western side.
2. [pass_summit | d0 | morning] Amagi Pass (34.8762, 138.9758) — The main crossing of the Amagi Mountains, 800m. Dense cedar forest. Serene and quiet.
3. [scenic | d0 | morning] Nishi-Izu Skyline Ridge (34.9350, 138.7200) — The ridge road runs high above the coast with Pacific views west and mountain views east. Best section of the day.
4. [photo | d0 | afternoon] Dogashima Cliffs & Sea Caves (34.8419, 138.7680) — Tuff-rock sea stacks and a famous cave accessed by boat. The rock formations are unlike anything else in Japan.
5. [food | d0 | afternoon] Matsuzaki (34.7333, 138.7667) — Small town famous for its namako-kabe walls (black and white tiles) and sakura-ebi (cherry shrimp) dishes.
6. [end | d0 | afternoon] Shimoda (34.6750, 138.9456) — End in Shimoda. Where Commodore Perry forced Japan to open to the West in 1854. Good fish market and harbour.

### Fuji Five Lakes Circuit — Yamanashi
Meta: JP | JP-19 | Kawaguchiko | 1d | 100km | moderate | paved | 950m↑ | pick:yes
Start: 35.5117, 138.7608
Description: A circuit of the five lakes formed by Mt Fuji's past eruptions. Each lake offers a different angle of the volcano on clear days. The Aokigahara forest (Sea of Trees) spreads across a vast lava field on Fuji's northwest flank. The Subaru Line toll road climbs to the 5th Station at 2,305m.

Waypoints:
1. [start | d0 | morning] Kawaguchiko (35.5117, 138.7608) — Start at Lake Kawaguchi — most accessible and most photographed lake. Fuji looms directly south.
2. [photo | d0 | morning] Kawaguchiko North Shore Fuji View (35.5300, 138.7500) — The classic Fuji + torii gate reflection. Best before 10am before clouds build.
3. [scenic | d0 | morning] Lake Saiko (35.5078, 138.6686) — Western lake. Quieter, wilder shores. Bat Cave and lava formations nearby.
4. [pass_summit | d0 | morning] Fuji Subaru Line — 5th Station 2,305m (35.4100, 138.7400) — Toll road to Fuji's 5th Station. Above the treeline, above the clouds on many days. Closes in winter.
5. [scenic | d0 | afternoon] Aokigahara Forest (35.4750, 138.6350) — The 'Sea of Trees' spreading across a 35km² lava field. Dense, twisted, and eerily silent. Stay on marked paths.
6. [photo | d0 | afternoon] Shiraito Falls (35.3875, 138.5998) — UNESCO Natural Monument. A wide curtain waterfall fed by Fuji's snowmelt seeping through volcanic rock for decades.
7. [end | d0 | afternoon] Fujinomiya (35.2236, 138.6173) — End in Fujinomiya at the foot of Fuji's south side. Best yakisoba (B-class gourmet).

### Hokkaido Akan-Mashu National Park Circuit
Meta: JP | JP-01 | Kushiro | 1d | 110km | moderate | paved | 720m↑ | pick:no
Start: 43.2333, 144.3833
Description: A volcanic lake circuit through Akan-Mashu National Park — one of Hokkaido's most remote and spectacular corners. Lake Mashu is one of the world's clearest lakes (visibility 41m), almost always hidden by mist. Lake Akan is surrounded by boiling pools and home to rare marimo algae balls.

Waypoints:
1. [start | d0 | morning] Kushiro (43.2333, 144.3833) — Start in Kushiro. Follow Route 241 northwest into the national park.
2. [scenic | d0 | morning] Lake Kussharo (43.6167, 144.3500) — Japan's largest caldera lake. Hot springs bubble up on the shore. Swans overwinter here.
3. [photo | d0 | morning] Lake Mashu — 1st Lookout (43.5790, 144.5335) — Japan's clearest lake, 205m deep, rarely visible — mist fills the caldera most mornings. When it clears: extraordinary.
4. [scenic | d0 | afternoon] Io-zan (Sulphur Mountain) (43.5000, 144.5167) — Active sulphur vents that you can walk among. Yellow sulphur deposits and steaming ground. Smell is intense.
5. [food | d0 | afternoon] Lake Akan Onsen Village (43.4455, 144.3766) — Traditional Ainu village on the lake shore. Try Ainu cuisine (venison, salmon) at lakeside restaurants.
6. [end | d0 | afternoon] Lake Akan Panorama (43.4800, 144.4500) — Hilltop panorama above the lake. Return to Kushiro via Route 240 through the Akan River wetlands.
